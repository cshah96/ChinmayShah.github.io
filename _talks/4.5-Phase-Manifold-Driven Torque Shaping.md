This work extends Fourier Latent Dynamics (FLD) by using its continuous, unsupervised latent state to directly shape exoskeleton assistance. Rather than mapping sensor readings to a fixed torque profile, the controller uses the user’s phase, frequency, and amplitude on the learned manifold to determine when and how much assistance to provide.

Because the latent phase evolves continuously across walking, stairs, ramps, and transitions, assistance can adapt smoothly without task classifiers or explicit heel-strike segmentation. The latent amplitude captures movement intensity, while the learned representation enables lightweight personalization to each user’s gait. Distance from the manifold provides an additional measure of confidence, allowing the controller to reduce assistance when motion falls outside familiar patterns.

Together, these capabilities move toward intuitive, adaptive exoskeleton control that continuously responds to the user’s motion state, rather than switching between predefined assistance strategies.
